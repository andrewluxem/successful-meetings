# Question bank

Good questions do more to steer a meeting than any agenda item. Pick 3 to 5 per meeting, matched to the meeting's job. Do not attach the whole bank; a packet with twenty questions is a packet with none.

## Who: accountability and stakeholders

Use when responsibilities are fuzzy or a decision's blast radius is unclear.

- Who benefits from this?
- Who is most directly affected?
- Who needs to be involved for this to stick?
- Who owns this once the meeting ends?

## What: objectives and stakes

Use early in a project, or whenever the goal has gone soft.

- What are we actually solving for?
- What is the best case and the worst case?
- What is getting in the way of action?
- What would make this a clear yes or a clear no?

## Where: focus and resources

Use when discussing strategy, scope, or allocation.

- Where is this most needed?
- Where will this idea take us?
- Where are our resources concentrated today?
- Where are the areas for improvement?

## When: timing and checkpoints

Use for planning and for keeping follow-ups honest.

- When is the best time to act?
- When will we know we have succeeded?
- When should we reassess?
- When is the real deadline, and what sets it?

## Why: rationale and assumptions

The sharpest tools in the bank. Use to test reasoning before committing.

- Why is this a problem worth solving?
- Why now?
- Why this approach over the alternatives?
- Why has this persisted despite earlier attempts?

## How: method and measurement

Use in planning phases and when evaluating feasibility.

- How will we implement this?
- How will we measure success?
- How does this affect the team's existing commitments?
- How would this fail?

## Matching guide

| Meeting job | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Make a decision | Why, What |
| Plan work | How, When |
| Assign ownership | Who |
| Review progress | When, What |
| Explore an idea | Where, Why |
